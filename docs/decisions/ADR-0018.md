# ADR-0018: Time, dates, and calendars

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-02
**Accepted:** 2026-08-08 — ratified as wave W3 (data foundation).
**Added beyond the supplied decision set** — timestamp semantics are effectively impossible to fix once data exists, and this system operates in Saudi Arabia where the Hijri calendar is a genuine requirement. The old system's specification called for "Hijri + Gregorian" support without stating where the conversion belongs.

## Context

Three distinct concerns are routinely conflated and must not be:

1. **Instants** — when something happened. Global, absolute, orderable.
2. **Calendar dates** — a due date, a tender deadline, a licence expiry. These are *not* instants; "20 August 2026" does not have a timezone.
3. **Calendar systems** — Gregorian and Hijri are two ways of naming the same day. Hijri dates in Saudi business documents are common and sometimes legally required.

Storing local wall-clock times without a zone is the classic unrecoverable mistake: once written, the original instant cannot be reconstructed. Storing a Hijri string as the canonical value is worse, because Hijri↔Gregorian conversion is calendar-authority dependent (Umm al-Qura) and not purely arithmetic.

The server runs in UTC; users are in Asia/Riyadh (UTC+3, no DST). SLA calculations, working-day arithmetic, and "overdue" determinations must not drift.

## Decision

- **Instants** are stored as `timestamptz` and are always UTC. Never `timestamp` without a zone.
- **Calendar dates** are stored as `date`. A tender deadline or a due date is a date, not midnight in some zone.
- **The canonical calendar is Gregorian.** Hijri is a **presentation format only**, converted at the edge (UI or PDF), never stored as the source of truth.
- **Business timezone** is a configuration value (`Asia/Riyadh`), stored in Settings, used for: rendering, day-boundary determination for SLA and "due today" logic, and working-day/holiday arithmetic.
- Derived temporal states (`OVERDUE`, `DUE_TODAY`) are **computed at read time**, not stored — this is C19 in the conflict register, where stored derived states went stale between cron runs.
- Holiday and working-day calendars live in Settings and are used by SLA calculations rather than being hardcoded.

## Consequences

**Positive**
- Instants are unambiguous and comparable regardless of where a client or server sits.
- The Hijri requirement is satisfied without contaminating storage or query semantics.
- C19 cannot recur: a derived state that is never stored cannot be stale.
- Changing business hours or adding a holiday is configuration, not a deployment.

**Negative / trade-offs**
- Every "today" question requires the business timezone rather than the server's, and forgetting it produces off-by-one-day bugs around midnight UTC (3am Riyadh). The lint rule below is the mitigation.
- Hijri conversion requires a library with Umm al-Qura support, and conversions can differ by a day from other authorities. Documented as a known limitation rather than solved.
- Computing `OVERDUE` at read time means it cannot be indexed directly; where a query must filter on it, the predicate is expressed against `due_at` instead, which is indexable.

## Rejected alternatives

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| **`timestamp` without time zone** | The original instant becomes unrecoverable. The single most common and least reversible date mistake. |
| **Store local Riyadh time** | Same problem, plus it breaks the moment any user, server, or integration sits elsewhere. |
| **Store Hijri as canonical** | Conversion is authority-dependent and not purely arithmetic; date arithmetic and range queries become unreliable. |
| **Store both Gregorian and Hijri columns** | Two sources of truth that can disagree — the same class of defect as C16. Convert at render time instead. |
| **Store `OVERDUE` / `DUE_TODAY` as columns updated by cron** | Precisely C19: stale between runs, and the staleness is invisible to the reader. |

## Enforcement

- **Migration lint** (ADR-0014): a `timestamp` column without a time zone fails CI. Date-like columns must be `date` or `timestamptz`, never `timestamp`.
- **Container and database configuration**: `TZ=UTC` on every container; PostgreSQL `timezone = 'UTC'`. Asserted by a startup check that fails boot if the process timezone is not UTC.
- **ESLint rule**: direct construction of "now" (`new Date()`, `Date.now()`) inside `domain/` is forbidden; a `Clock` port is injected instead, which also makes time-dependent domain logic testable without waiting.
- **ESLint rule**: date formatting or day-boundary arithmetic that does not pass an explicit timezone is an error.
- **Test**: SLA and "due today" suites run with the system clock set to 23:30 Riyadh (20:30 UTC) to catch day-boundary errors.
- **No stored derived state**: migration lint rejects columns named `is_overdue`, `due_today`, or similar on tables that also carry a `due_at`.
