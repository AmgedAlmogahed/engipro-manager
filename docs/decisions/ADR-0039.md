# ADR-0039: ZATCA e-invoicing applicability

**Status:** Proposed — **BLOCKED** on open question: *does Abak issue invoices to entities inside Saudi Arabia?*
**Date:** 2026-08-02

## Context

Saudi Arabia's Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA) mandates e-invoicing (*Fatoorah*) for resident taxable persons. Phase 1 (generation) required structured electronic invoices with specific fields and a QR code. Phase 2 (integration) requires invoices to be cryptographically signed, formatted as XML (UBL 2.1), reported or cleared through ZATCA's platform, and to carry a compliant QR code.

Abak is an engineering consultancy operating in Saudi Arabia. If it invoices Saudi entities, this is not optional and it is not a late-stage feature.

**Why this must be answered before Finance is built:** ZATCA compliance shapes the invoice data model (additional mandatory fields: seller and buyer VAT registration numbers, commercial registration, invoice type codes, line-item tax categories, invoice counter value, previous invoice hash), the invoice numbering rules (ADR-0025), the cryptographic signing and archival requirements, and — critically — **the PDF worker's output contract** (ADR-0032), because the QR code and the compliant layout are part of the document, not decoration.

Retrofitting these fields into an invoice table that already holds issued invoices means either a migration of legally significant records or a parallel invoice model. Both are bad.

## Decision

**BLOCKED.** Two outcomes, both fully specified so this can be closed quickly:

**If Abak invoices Saudi entities (expected):**
- Finance's invoice aggregate carries ZATCA-mandated fields from its first migration, even if the signing and reporting integration ships later.
- Invoice numbering follows ZATCA sequencing rules and uses the counter mechanism of ADR-0025.
- The PDF worker's output contract includes the QR code and compliant layout from the first version.
- Signed XML generation and ZATCA platform integration are treated as an **Integrations** anti-corruption layer (ADR-0006) with its own adapter, scheduled at Phase 5.
- Invoice retention aligns with ADR-0042.

**If Abak does not invoice Saudi entities:**
- This ADR is marked **Rejected**, recording the explicit decision and its date, so that the absence of ZATCA support is a documented choice rather than an oversight.

## Consequences

**If applicable and implemented from the start** — a somewhat wider invoice model and a more constrained PDF contract, in exchange for avoiding a migration of legally significant records.

**If applicable and deferred** — invoices issued before compliance are non-compliant records that may need reissuing, and the PDF contract changes after documents have already been sent to clients.

**If not applicable** — a simpler invoice model; the explicit rejection prevents someone re-raising it in a year with no record of why it was skipped.

## Rejected alternatives

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| **Build a generic invoice model and adapt later** | The additional fields are mandatory and some are cryptographically chained (invoice counter and previous-invoice hash). A chain cannot be retrofitted onto records issued before it existed. |
| **Use a third-party invoicing service for KSA compliance** | A legitimate option that should be evaluated *if* the answer is yes — it would sit behind an Integrations adapter. Not a reason to defer the data model decision. |
| **Assume it applies and build it regardless** | Wasted work and a permanently more complex invoice model if Abak does not invoice Saudi entities. |

## Enforcement

- **Blocking**: no Finance invoice migration may be written while this ADR is `BLOCKED`. Enforced by the mining-document rule (ADR-0003) — `docs/mined/finance.md` cannot be completed without resolving this.
- Once resolved: contract tests assert every mandated field is present and non-null on issued invoices; a PDF golden-file test asserts the QR code is present and decodes to the expected payload.
- If rejected: this file's Status becomes `Rejected` with a date and rationale, which is itself the enforcement — a future reader finds the decision rather than the gap.
