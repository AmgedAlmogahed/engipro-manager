# ADR-0042: Audit and financial record retention

**Status:** Proposed — **BLOCKED** on open question E: *confirm required retention periods*
**Date:** 2026-08-02

## Context

ADR-0024 establishes an append-only audit log that is never deleted by the application. That is correct for integrity and unsustainable forever without a retention policy: the table grows monotonically, and a policy decided after ten years of data is a policy applied to data that may already have been kept too long or not long enough.

Two distinct retention questions:

1. **Financial and tax records.** Saudi tax regulations impose minimum retention periods on invoices, tax records, and supporting documents. If ZATCA e-invoicing applies (ADR-0039), its own retention requirements apply to e-invoice data. Commonly cited figures are **6 years** for tax records and longer for core accounting records, but the precise obligation for Abak's circumstances must be confirmed by whoever advises them on tax and corporate compliance — not inferred from a blog post or from this document.

2. **Operational audit entries.** Permission changes, status transitions, and access logs have no external mandate but do have practical value for dispute resolution.

## Decision

**BLOCKED** pending confirmation. Proposed defaults, stated so the decision can be closed quickly once verified:

| Category | Proposed retention |
|---|---|
| Invoices, payments, tax records, e-invoice data | **10 years** |
| Financial audit entries (approvals, validations, PO issuance) | **10 years** |
| Operational audit entries (status transitions, permission changes) | **6 years** |
| Access and read audit entries | **2 years** |
| Notification delivery logs | **1 year** |
| Outbox rows | Deleted on success (ADR-0026) — not a retention category |
| Application logs | 30–90 days (ADR-0036) — not audit data |

**Hash chaining is deferred.** ADR-0024's schema reserves room for a `prev_hash` column so tamper-evidence can be added without redesign. It is warranted only if a legal or contractual requirement for tamper-evidence emerges; hash chaining serialises audit writes, which is acceptable at this scale but is a cost with no current justification.

**Deletion of audit data is a separate, audited administrative operation** performed by a distinct database role — never an application feature, consistent with ADR-0023.

## Consequences

**Positive**
- Retention becomes a deliberate, documented decision rather than "we kept everything and hoped."
- Tiering by category avoids applying the strictest requirement to the highest-volume data.
- Deferring hash chaining while preserving the schema shape keeps the option cheap.

**Negative / trade-offs**
- Multi-year retention on a single VM means the audit table becomes one of the largest objects in the database. Partitioning by month, and archiving cold partitions to object storage (ADR-0034), will be needed before the ten-year horizon.
- Any deletion of audit data is a sensitive operation and a plausible attack vector; the separate role and audited procedure are the mitigation.
- Retention periods stated here are **proposals from general knowledge and must be verified**. Acting on unverified compliance figures is itself a risk.

## Rejected alternatives

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| **Keep everything forever** | Simple and defensible for integrity, but unbounded growth on a single VM, and some regulations impose maximum retention on personal data as well as minimums. |
| **Uniform retention across all categories** | Either over-retains high-volume operational data or under-retains financial records. |
| **Delete audit entries from the application** | Contradicts ADR-0024's append-only guarantee and would require granting `DELETE` on the audit schema. |
| **Hash chaining now** | Serialises audit writes for a tamper-evidence property nobody has yet required. The schema keeps the option open. |

## Enforcement

- **Blocking**: no retention or archival job is implemented while this ADR is `BLOCKED`. Until then, nothing is deleted — the safe default.
- Once resolved: a scheduled archival job moves partitions older than the retention period to object storage and records the operation in the audit log itself.
- **Partitioning**: `audit.audit_log` is partitioned by month from the first migration, so archival later is a partition detach rather than a mass delete. **This is a now decision even though retention is blocked** — retrofitting partitioning to a large table is expensive.
- **Grants**: the application role holds `INSERT, SELECT` only; archival runs as a separate role (ADR-0024).
- **Verification**: retention figures must be confirmed with Abak's tax or legal advisor and the source recorded in this ADR before it moves to Accepted.
